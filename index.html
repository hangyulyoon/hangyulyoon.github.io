I am a physician working on machine learning for medicine — teaching vision-language models to read chest X-rays the way a clinician does, and building benchmarks that test whether large language models can actually reason over real patient records.

I am an MD-PhD student at the Kim Jaechul Graduate School of AI at KAIST, working with Prof. Eunho Yang and collaborating closely with Prof. Edward Choi's group and the Microsoft Research Health Futures team. My research centers on multimodal medical AI: sentence-level vision-language pretraining, instruction-guided lesion segmentation, one-shot adaptation of foundation models to medical images, and structured, longitudinal evaluation of radiology report generation.

Before moving into AI, my research was in radiation oncology, where I studied sarcopenia and treatment response in thoracic cancers and built early machine-learning models to predict muscle loss during chemoradiotherapy. That clinical background shapes how I think about what a model needs to get right before it is useful at the bedside.
